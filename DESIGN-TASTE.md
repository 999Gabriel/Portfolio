# Gabriel Winkler — Design Taste

A detailed analysis of your design sensibility, reverse-engineered from the portfolio
and from every decision and correction made while building it. Think of this as a
style DNA you can hand to any designer, tool, or agent so they build something that
feels like *you* on the first try.

---

## 1. The one-sentence version

> **Ruthless minimalism with a warm, editorial soul — say the least possible, say it
> beautifully, and never sound like you're selling.**

Everything below is a consequence of that sentence.

---

## 2. Core principles (in your own logic)

### 2.1 Subtraction over addition
Your instinct is to *remove*. When something felt "overbuilt," the fix was never
"add polish" — it was "delete the element." You stripped eyebrows, status pills,
meta tickers, section numbers, buttons, a tech marquee, and photo badges. The
question you implicitly ask every element is: **"Does this earn its place, or is it
noise pretending to be design?"** Most things are noise.

### 2.2 Anti-slop radar
You have a low tolerance for what you literally called "slop." Slop, to you, is:
- **Fake signals of importance** — a blinking "live" dot, a "PORTFOLIO — 2026" label,
  numbers like `19 / HTL / MATURA 2027` stacked like a stock ticker. Decoration that
  imitates function.
- **Marketing voice** — "I build things that *actually ship*," "LET'S build it."
  You said it plainly: *"I'm not selling anything, this isn't a marketing business
  site."* Copy that performs enthusiasm reads as insecure to you.
- **Performative identity** — you removed the word *"founder"* because you "haven't
  founded anything yet." Accuracy beats flattery. You'd rather undersell than posture.

### 2.3 Facts over fluff
The purpose of the site, in your words: *"to get to know me and who I am."* So the
content is **declarative and true**: age, place, school, what you're building, what
you've done and when. No adjectives doing PR work. When a fact was wrong (ORBIS
mislabeled, CULINA's dates, the wrong study branch), fixing it mattered more than any
visual flourish. **Truth is a design value for you, not just an editorial one.**

### 2.4 Beauty is not optional — it's the point
This is the crucial nuance that keeps your minimalism from being cold. Your own line:
> *"Even in software, you have to search for beauty."*

You care about aesthetics, branding, and **how a thing feels**, drawn from an interest
in fashion. So the minimalism is not austerity for its own sake — it's *taste as
restraint*. Empty space is a material you use on purpose. The gap between elements is
as designed as the elements.

---

## 3. Reference & lineage

Your north star is **Julian Melgers** (julianmelgers.com) — but explicitly for
*"the vibe and look and feel,"* not the words or structure. What you pulled from it:

- A near-empty first screen: a centered name, a tiny caption, and an ocean of white.
- Content that arrives as **sparse, left-aligned statements** with huge vertical gaps —
  each line given room to breathe and be read alone.
- A quiet, almost anonymous footer. No hard sell, no CTA blast.

This places you in a **fashion-editorial / gallery** lineage rather than a
"tech portfolio" one. You'd rather look like a lookbook than a landing page.

---

## 4. Visual language

### 4.1 Color — monochrome, warm
- Warm off-white paper (`#FCFBF8`) and near-black ink (`#17140F`). **No** brand color,
  no accent hue, no gradients, no shadows.
- You rejected the earlier warm-cream + crimson scheme as "vibe coded." The palette is
  now essentially **paper and ink**, with opacity (not new colors) creating hierarchy:
  full ink for what matters, muted/faint for support.
- **Color is reserved for the work itself.** Project shots and certificates render in
  grayscale and **reveal their real color on hover** — restraint by default, richness
  on intent.

### 4.2 Typography — a deliberate three-voice system
You chose the fonts yourself, and each has a job:
- **Balboa (bold "Fill" cut)** — your name and display. Condensed, confident, uppercase.
  This is the structural, architectural voice.
- **Shadows Into Light** — the human signature. Used for the hero caption and the footer
  signature. It's the handwritten, personal counterweight to Balboa's rigidity.
- **Cormorant Garamond (serif)** — the body and captions. Elegant, literary, calm. This
  is what makes the site feel editorial rather than technical.

The pairing logic is telling: **a hard geometric display + a handwritten accent + a
refined serif for reading.** Structure, soul, and substance. You deliberately avoided
monospace/technical type — no "developer" cosplay.

### 4.3 Layout & space
- **Whitespace is the primary design element.** Sections are separated by large,
  confident padding. Nothing is crowded.
- **Hairline rules only** — 1px lines at ~12% opacity. **Sharp corners**, never rounded.
  No cards, no boxes, no elevation. Flat and precise.
- **Centered hero, left-aligned everything else.** The name is a monument in the middle
  of emptiness; the substance reads like a column of quiet notes.
- Alignment and rhythm carry the design where decoration would in a busier site.

### 4.4 Motion & interaction
- **Barely-there.** A soft fade-in on load; a quiet underline that grows on hover.
  Nothing bouncy, nothing attention-seeking.
- You have zero patience for motion that *hides content* — when a scroll animation left
  the Work section invisible, that was an immediate, non-negotiable bug.
- The one "signature" interaction is tasteful, not flashy: hovering a project name
  **previews its image** in the negative space. Interaction that reveals, never
  interrupts.

---

## 5. Voice & copy

- **Terse, lowercase-feeling, factual.** Short declarative lines. No exclamation-energy.
- **First-person but understated.** "19, from Wattens. I build software across AI,
  full-stack, and hardware." Confident because it's plain, not because it's loud.
- **No CTAs, no persuasion.** The footer is a name, a few links, a copyright — an
  invitation to reach out, not a funnel.
- **Precision in naming** — real titles, real dates, real tools (.NET MAUI, Three.js,
  FastAPI). Specificity is a form of honesty you respect.

---

## 6. What you consistently reject

A quick "do-not" list distilled from your corrections:

- ❌ Status/"live" indicators and decorative pills
- ❌ Stacked micro-labels and ticker-style metadata
- ❌ Marketing slogans, hype verbs, and CTA pressure
- ❌ Performative or aspirational self-labels that aren't true yet
- ❌ Brand colors used as decoration, gradients, drop shadows, rounded "app" cards
- ❌ Monospace/"techy" styling for its own sake
- ❌ Animations that delay or hide content
- ❌ Density and clutter — anything that feels "vibe coded"

---

## 7. What you consistently reward

- ✅ Emptiness used with confidence
- ✅ One strong idea per screen
- ✅ Type doing the heavy lifting (display + handwriting + serif)
- ✅ Monochrome discipline, with color as a considered exception
- ✅ Truthful, specific, minimal copy
- ✅ Subtle, revealing interactions
- ✅ A personal, human touch (your handwritten signature) inside an austere frame
- ✅ Craft details: hairlines, precise spacing, sharp edges

---

## 8. The underlying worldview

If you zoom out, your taste is coherent because it comes from a single belief:

> **Confidence is quiet. You don't decorate what's already good — you clear space
> around it so it can be seen. Beauty and honesty are the same discipline.**

It's a fashion designer's sensibility applied to software: know exactly what to leave
out, treat white space and typography as luxury materials, refuse to shout, and let
taste — not features — be the differentiator.

---

## 9. Practical brief (for reuse)

If you ever brief someone in ten lines:

1. Warm white background, near-black text. No other colors.
2. Balboa for the name/headers, Shadows Into Light for signature/caption, Cormorant
   Garamond serif for body.
3. Huge whitespace. Hairline rules. Sharp corners. No shadows/gradients/cards.
4. Centered hero: photo, name, one small caption. Then near-emptiness.
5. Everything else: sparse, left-aligned, factual lines with big gaps.
6. Grayscale imagery that reveals color on hover.
7. Copy = true facts, terse, first person, zero marketing.
8. Motion = subtle fades and underlines only; never hide content.
9. One quiet signature interaction is allowed; nothing flashy.
10. When in doubt, remove it.
